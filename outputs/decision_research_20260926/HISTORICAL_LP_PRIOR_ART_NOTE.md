# Historical mass-balance bounds: additional novelty constraint

Checked 2026-09-26 UTC. This is a bounded bibliographic addendum, not a new
experiment or an exhaustive priority search. It does not change any result.

The official EPA 2004 *Protocol for Applying and Validating the CMB Model for
PM2.5 and VOC*, EPA-451/R-04-001, section 1.3, already lists linear programming
among CMB solution methods and attributes that route to Hougland (1983). Its
bibliography names *Chemical element balance by linear programming*. The
conference paper itself was not recovered; its exact formulation, uncertainty
treatment and available proofs remain uninspected. Cite the inspected EPA
protocol for this historical statement, not an invented DOI or a claim to have
read Hougland. [Official protocol](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=P1009QZM.TXT).

Braun's *Quantitative Analysis of Mineral Mixtures Using Linear Programming*,
*Clays and Clay Minerals* 34(3), 330–337 (1986), is adjacent-domain primary
prior art. Its publisher abstract describes approximate, model-dependent
phase-quantity bounds from bulk chemical composition and mineral-composition
ranges, with stoichiometric and X-ray constraints that can narrow the
alternatives. It also describes an example
in which chemically indistinguishable phases are identified only as their
sum. The publisher's 2024 online-posting date must not be mistaken for the
original publication year. Only the abstract and bibliographic metadata were
inspected; no fulltext or redistribution permission was obtained.
[Publisher/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1346/CCMN.1986.0340314).

## Consequence for this candidate

**REMOVE** any claim that linear programming for chemical mass balance,
min/max component bounds, or grouping indistinguishable components originates
in PFFLS. These sources reinforce the existing novelty audit; they do not
establish identity of every implementation detail or prove that the current
certificate workflow has already been published unchanged.

The candidate contribution remains a qualified, reproducible CMB decision
audit: explicitly declared uncertainty sets, separately checkable witnesses
and proof gaps, real historical profile alternatives, fair conventional
comparators, and preserved negative controls. Scientific or practical benefit
must come from the verified findings, not the age or name of the optimizer.
No paper was downloaded or added to a redistribution package for this note.
